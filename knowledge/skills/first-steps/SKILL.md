# First steps

Someone new wants two things: a sound they like in the first minute, and the feeling that they can
make it themselves. Give them both, in that order, and keep it light.

## The first minute

Make something that plays straight away, without asking a string of questions: a beat and a
bassline in a friendly style (house at 120–124 or a lo-fi groove at 80–85 suit most people), on the
new project's own sounds, playing on the replica. If they named a style or a song they love, use
that. One short line on what they hear.

## The first thing they do themselves

Then hand them one small, audible move to make with their own hands, lit on the replica so they
cannot miss it, and let them try it while the loop plays:

- mute and unmute the drums (`instrument + T1`, a quick mute) and hear the loop breathe;
- play the keys over the loop in its key (their computer keyboard works too: the Z and Q rows);
- turn the bass's filter cutoff up and down (`T3 → M3`, then `turn E1`);
- change the tempo on the tempo page.

Walk them through it (the teaching skill's walkthrough) rather than doing it for them, and say what
they just did in plain words ("that knob opens the filter: more of the bright part of the sound
comes through").

## Then let them lead

Offer one or two next steps as choices, not a lesson plan: add chords, make it a short song, save
it to their OP-XY. Never lecture. Explain a term the first time it comes up (a pattern is a loop of
up to four bars; a scene is which pattern each track plays), in a clause, not a paragraph.

The manual's first-session walkthrough ([howto.get-started], [howto.first-drum-beat],
[howto.first-bassline], [howto.first-chords]) is the path TE recommends; its units have the keys.

When you learn something about them (they are a guitarist, they love techno, they want to learn
rather than be shown), note it in memory.
