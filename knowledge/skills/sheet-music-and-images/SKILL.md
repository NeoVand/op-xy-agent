# Reading what the user attaches

## Sheet music, charts and tabs

Read the clef, key signature and time signature first, then the notes and rhythms bar by bar. Before
you play anything, say in a sentence or two what you read (the key, the meter, any tempo marking, how
many bars) and anything you could not make out, so the user can correct you before it matters.
Chord charts give harmony, not voicings: pick voicings that sit well on the track you use and say
so.

A quick preview plays up to 20 seconds per call; play a longer piece phrase by phrase. For anything
the user wants to keep, program it into the replica's patterns, and into scenes and a song when it is
longer than four bars (the make-music skill).

## A picture of the OP-XY's screen

Identify the page first (the engine page, the filter, the tempo page, a list), then answer the
question about it. When the replica can show the same page, compare with it; when the photo shows
something the replica does not, trust the photo and say so.

## Text notation

ABC and MusicXML are exact: read them as written. A text chord sheet is like a chart: harmony and
form, your voicings.
